---
title: "ACC Frequently Asked Questions"
description: "Answers to common questions about the Architectural Control Committee, deed restrictions, setbacks, easements, sheds, fences, and building permits in Knollwood Village."
---

<div class="faq">

<details><summary>What is the ACC?</summary><div>The Architectural Control Committee is established by the deed restrictions to enforce the restrictions that govern the size of houses, the materials used in construction, and the location of houses, buildings, and fences on each lot.</div></details>

<details><summary>Who is on the ACC?</summary><div>The ACC is a committee of the Knollwood Village Civic Club. Members are all volunteers approved by the club's Board of Directors. See the current members on the <a href="/contact/">contact page</a>.</div></details>

<details><summary>Are the deed restrictions the same for every section?</summary><div>The restrictions for all sections except Section 8 are very similar but differ slightly. Always consult the restrictions for the section your property is in. Section 8's restrictions were modified by a majority vote of its homeowners in September 2020. Find every section, and a map to locate yours, on the <a href="/deeds/">deed restrictions page</a>.</div></details>

<details><summary>When am I required to submit drawings for ACC approval?</summary><div>Any work that changes the size or shape of your home should be reviewed and approved before work begins. Re-roofing, painting, and landscape work do not require approval.</div></details>

<details><summary>What do I need to submit for project approval?</summary><div>The list of drawings, documents, and formats required for committee review is on the <a href="/docs/acc/Plan-Approval-Submission-Form.pdf">Plan Approval Submission Form</a>. See the <a href="/build/">ACC process</a> for the steps.</div></details>

<details><summary>What happens if I don't get ACC approval?</summary><div>Any City of Houston building permit application requires the homeowner to sign an affidavit affirming that the work complies with all deed restrictions for the neighborhood. Without ACC approval, a homeowner would have to submit a false permit application and would be open to prosecution by the City Attorney.</div></details>

<details><summary>Can I appeal the findings of the ACC?</summary><div>Yes. Under the club's bylaws, any homeowner whose project is rejected by the ACC can appeal to the Board of Directors for a vote.</div></details>

<details><summary>What counts as a story?</summary><div>See the <a href="/docs/acc/Guideline-for-Multi-Story-Residences-2020.pdf">Guideline for Multi-Story Residences</a>. It does not apply to Section 8 or Braes Terrace II, which have their <a href="/docs/acc/Building-Guidelines-Section-8.pdf">own building guidelines</a>.</div></details>

<details><summary>Do I need to contact the ACC for a storage shed?</summary><div>Yes. Storage sheds, out-buildings, and separate garages must be reviewed and approved by the ACC. Under the deed restrictions of many sections, detached structures have different setbacks from the main residence.</div></details>

<details><summary>Can I build into the utility easement?</summary><div>There are two types of easements on most properties. The 5-foot utility easement (U.E.) adjacent to many rear lot lines can never be built in and must be kept clear. Easements labeled A.E. reserve the air space from 20 feet above the ground and up, which generally means a single-story portion of a building can extend into that easement. CenterPoint Energy, which has legal use of that space, may have additional restrictions on minimum distances from utility wires.</div></details>

<details><summary>Do I need to contact the ACC for a driveway or swimming pool?</summary><div>Generally not. But if the work requires a building permit, you may need ACC approval so you can sign the permit affidavit the City requires.</div></details>

<details><summary>Can I subdivide my property?</summary><div>According to the City of Houston, a lot can be subdivided if the resulting lots all meet the minimum lot size in the deed restrictions or, if none is specified, 5,000 square feet. Because this can be expensive and time-consuming, consult the ACC and the City of Houston Planning Department first.</div></details>

<details><summary>Can I build a house on two lots?</summary><div>The City of Houston will require you to re-plat the two lots into a single building site. Re-platting removes the previous interior building lines and redefines the property as one site. Utility easements cannot be removed by a replat or subdivision.</div></details>

<details><summary>How can I tell if my property is in the floodplain?</summary><div>Type your address into the <a href="https://msc.fema.gov/">FEMA flood map</a>. Zone AE is considered floodplain, and any new construction or addition there must be elevated above the base flood elevation.</div></details>

<details><summary>Can I install a fence in the front of my home?</summary><div>Texas law changed in 2021 (effective 2022) regarding front-yard fences and other residential security measures. See the club's <a href="/docs/deeds/Front-Fence-Guideline-2022.pdf">Front Fence Guideline</a> and the <a href="/docs/acc/Residential-Security-Measures-Guidelines-2022.pdf">Residential Security Measures Guidelines</a>.</div></details>

<details><summary>How tall a fence can I build?</summary><div>The deed restrictions do not address fence height. The City of Houston requires an engineer's seal and a permit for any fence taller than 8 feet above adjacent grade, or for any brick or masonry wall taller than 4 feet.</div></details>

<details><summary>What's up with the sidewalks?</summary><div><a href="/docs/acc/Whats-Up-With-Sidewalks.pdf">Here is the deal</a> on sidewalks in Knollwood Village.</div></details>

<details><summary>How do I find out if construction in the neighborhood was submitted and approved?</summary><div>Email <a href="mailto:build@knollwoodvillage.org">build@knollwoodvillage.org</a> and we will let you know.</div></details>
