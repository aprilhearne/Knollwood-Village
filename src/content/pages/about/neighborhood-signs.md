## Project history (2014 to 2015)

At the Civic Club's August 2014 residents meeting, the board checked the pulse of the neighborhood on our esplanade signs. An overwhelming majority supported replacing the signs located on Buffalo Speedway and S. Braeswood, pictured below.

A rendering of a proposed new sign was displayed and feedback collected at that meeting. Volunteers formed a Beautification sub-committee to revise the design using that feedback and to look for ways to reduce the cost without sacrificing quality. The result was a fresh design unique among neighborhood signs in the area.

<img src="/images/signs/new-sign-rendering.jpg" alt="Artist rendering of the new Knollwood Village entrance sign" />

The design has a modern look with several nods to the neighborhood's history:

- The font, Bellerose, is a retro 1950s face representing when the neighborhood was established.
- "Est. 1951" is when the Knollwood Village Civic Club was established. Homes began being built in 1951, and all of the sections that make up the neighborhood were established by 1954.
- A hill, or knoll, represents "Knollwood."
- A tree symbolizes the mature trees that make the neighborhood so beautiful.
- The colors and aluminum materials give a modern look and avoid making the signs look like headstones.
- The rooftop shape of the sign and the tops of the brick columns say "residential neighborhood," and the brick echoes many of the original homes.
- The materials should last about 25 years maintenance-free, and with a little care another 25 after that. The paint is essentially automotive grade and coated to resist graffiti.

The new signs mark the boundaries of the neighborhood, present a positive image, convey that the neighborhood is protected by deed restrictions, give it a sense of permanence and identity within the city, and are clear and easy to read without distracting passing motorists.

Special thanks to the residents who participated in the final design: Emily Anmuth, David Fitts, Bert Magh, Reed Hablinski, Liz Devaney, Parul Vyas, Brian Warwick, Hector Caram, Brent Nyquist, and Scott Rose.

## Cost and fundraising

The original design was estimated at about $31,000 including demolition of the existing signs, permitting, construction, and installation of two new double-sided signs. By January 2015 the permitting process was complete and the final cost had come down to $22,875. The club also applied for and was awarded a City of Houston grant of $2,845.13 toward the project, with the balance raised from residents. The signs were installed in 2015 and are maintained by the Beautification Committee.
